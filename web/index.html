Milestones + scenario clock

Milestone board

    1x = one scenario day per 12 seconds. Pausing stops the clock and all costs.

    Work packages

    Dispatch once; crews work when predecessors, materials and equipment are ready. Priority puts a package first in the shared-resource queue. Hold parks that assignment without resetting its work.

    Staffing + costs Idle resources accrue cost; additions bill two days up front.

    Add a second crew to open another work front. One crew does one package at a time. Remove finished trades and idle equipment to stop their daily costs; add them again if another package needs them.

    Site traffic

    On: equipment waits for crossings and workers keep clear. Off: unprotected crossings can cause equipment contact and stop the entire site. A contact adds a $25,000 game allowance, a half-day review and a 10-point score penalty. These are game rules, not real incident costs or procedures.

    Equipment spreads

    Order the complete package before the crew needs it. Expediting costs 20% extra and shortens the remaining lead time by 45%. Materials are paid when ordered. All figures are scenario allowances.

    What this scenario represents

    One representative construction phase, with 12 ha of sitework, two primary structures and support yards. Building geometry is schematic. Hired crews are shown as individual workers. Machines and commuter vehicles represent the larger spreads; movement follows illustrative routes.

    Costs include preset installed-work allowances, purchase packages, eight-hour labor shifts, rented equipment and $12,000/day site overhead. They are not an estimate for the real development. Game weather repeats on a 12-day cycle.

      Field guide / Campus construction

      You run the project.
      The crews run the equipment.

      Explore, then mobilize

      The world fills your window. Drag to rotate; use a wheel, trackpad, pinch or the zoom buttons to move closer. Focus the globe and use arrow keys to rotate and + / - to zoom. World resets the camera; US projects brings all seven scenarios into view. Choose a marker or a project in the list, read its brief, then mobilize. The site view also fills the window. Open Operations to manage work, labor and deliveries.

      1. Release the work. Start construction dispatches crews, orders the initial material packages and starts the clock. Use Operations for individual assignments and orders. Each crew starts when its predecessor packages are accepted. No repeated Space presses.
      2. Buy ahead. Open Procurement and order steel, power, cooling and fit-out packages. Their clocks run alongside construction.
      3. Follow the bottleneck. Read each package's waiting reason. A spare crane cannot replace an electrical crew. Add capacity under Labor + equipment, or prioritize an urgent package.
      4. Inspect before covering. Formation, utility acceptance, concrete-strength releases, pre-energization and owner handover need a release after their checks reach 100%. The superintendent's desk brings each release to you.
      5. Finish the whole project. The shell is only a milestone. Complete services, fit-out, integrated tests, restoration and records to hand over the site.

      Watch the work happen

      Follow work brings the camera to an active crew; Next crew moves to another work front. Dragging releases the camera. Excavators cut the terrain and load haul trucks; trucks tip at the fill and dozers spread it. A separate utility spread opens the trench, sets pipe and backfills behind the pipe crew. Foundation excavation is followed by forms, reinforcing, a pre-pour check and concrete placement. Curing uses elapsed time and frees the crew and pump. Cranes wait for a separate strength and anchor release. Carriers bring individual structural members and plant skids from laydown; cranes rig, hoist and connect those same pieces. Installed quantities advance at placement, before the empty carrier returns. Inside work is visible in Follow work or with Roof off.

      Parking shows the 32-space crew lot and the pedestrian route to check-in. On a fresh project, cars arrive during mobilization and workers walk from the lot. Hired trades remain at the compound while waiting for work. Vehicles and equipment represent their larger spreads; workers route around installed walls, columns and racks, using the goods doors. Traffic separation is on by default: equipment yields to pedestrians, and pedestrians wait before crossing. Turn it off under Site traffic to explore the consequences. Equipment contact stops construction and requires a review and release. Contacts are nongraphic; clearances and penalties are game assumptions, not a safety-training standard. Each scenario day takes 12 seconds at 1x; use 3x or 8x to advance faster.

      Construction process model

      The 44-package model separates productive work, elapsed waits and acceptance. Utility crews work in short reaches: excavate and install, inspect before covering, backfill and compact, then check density. An available survey/inspection crew performs those reach checks automatically. System-specific utility tests follow installation. Building and plant crews can work in parallel in separate areas.

      Installed systems pass pre-energization review, vendor startup, functional performance tests and integrated systems tests before records, training and handover. The three-day curing wait is a game allowance, not a prediction of concrete strength. Actual releases depend on the design, test evidence and responsible professionals.

      Sources behind the model

      What changes by destination

      Ground presets alter excavation duration. Rain slows outdoor work; wind holds cranes; heat reduces outdoor productivity. Terafab adds longer process-utility, cleanroom and qualification work. Starbase replaces a hall with a tower and civil pad. These are learning scenarios, not operating procedures.
